# Mahabharata: Dyuta Sabha — Modern Rules Proposal

**Status:** Interpreted rules for the Native, local-only **Dyuta Sabha** experience. The product owner has chosen a traditional presentation for Dyuta Sabha and reserved **Open Throw** as a separate future modern strategy game. Native exposes three disclosed Guide policies as Easy/Medium/Hard, plus cosmetic side/avatar/color choices. This implementation is not an approval of historical authenticity, specialist review, text/art rights, or public release. These are Shoonaya-designed mechanics, not a reconstruction of the Mahabharata's historical rules.

**Scope:** Native app only. Gyan Chaupar remains a separate deferred feature. For historical and source boundaries, see [Stage 0 research](MAHABHARATA_DYUTA_STAGE_0_RESEARCH.md) and the [Native scope](MAHABHARATA_DYUTA_NATIVE_SCOPE.md).

## Recommendation

Use the current short, five-round interpreted rules inside **Dyuta Sabha**: two ordinary six-sided dice and one optional reroll of a single die per round. The player competes against a transparent, non-character guide in solo mode; both use the same dice distribution and turn rules. There are no wagers, coins, purchasable rolls, streak penalties, prizes, player-to-player transfers, or spiritual scores.

Keep sourced story material separate from the repeatable match loop within the broader Dyuta Sabha experience. Do not reenact Yudhishthira's losses, use Shakuni as the opponent, or make Draupadi or any person's freedom, dignity, or safety a game object. The interface may use a traditional Sabha-inspired presentation, but its historical note must say that the playable rules are Shoonaya interpretation and are not presented as the epic's historical rules.

## Dyuta interpreted rules v1: MVP contract

### Match setup

- Modes: one player versus a neutral rules-based guide. The guide is fictional and must not be named or depicted as an epic character.
- Match length: five rounds, with one player turn per round for each side.
- Each side begins at zero. There is no entry fee, stake, transferable currency, reward with monetary value, or spiritual-progress reward.
- The guide's policy is disclosed in the help sheet: after its first roll, it rerolls its lower die if that value is 1 or 2; otherwise it keeps both. If both values are equal, it chooses the left die. Its dice are generated by the same fair mechanism as the player's.
- Provide a no-opponent practice option during prototype testing if needed to validate comprehension and accessibility independently of the guide.

### Turn sequence

1. Show whose turn it is and the round number (1–5).
2. Roll two six-sided dice once. Show both results and make them available to screen readers.
3. The player chooses **Keep** or selects exactly one die and chooses **Reroll**. Rerolling is optional and may happen only once in that turn. The other die stays unchanged.
4. Show the final two values and add their sum, 2–12, to that side's match total.
5. Record the action sequence locally: initial values, keep/reroll decision, final values, points, ruleset version, and turn number. Never describe the values as a historical Mahabharata procedure.
6. Repeat for the other side. Alternate which side takes the first turn in each round.
7. After round five, the higher total wins; equal totals are a draw. There is no sudden-death reroll.

The player's reroll action should not be timed. Animation may be skipped, reduced, or disabled; the result and available action remain identical.

### Scoring and difficulty

- A turn scores only the sum of the final two dice. Match total range is 10–60 across five rounds.
- **Current Native difficulty:** Easy rerolls the lower die on 1; Medium on 1 or 2; Hard on 1, 2, or 3. The chosen policy is shown before play and in Help. It changes only the Guide's disclosed decision threshold; both sides use identical independently generated dice and the same number of turns. These settings do not secretly alter luck, and results from different difficulties should not be compared as equivalent rankings.
- **Cosmetic identity:** Pandavas/Kauravas side labels, avatar emblems, and board colors identify seats only. They do not alter turns, die generation, score, or story outcomes, and must not be marketed as authentic teams in a historical reconstruction.
- Show the guide policy before a match and expose it again from pause/help.
- No global high-score table in the MVP. A local best result may be saved on device, clearly labelled local and resettable. Do not compare solo scores across guide styles as if they were equivalent.

### Quit, interruption, and replay

- Pause is always available. Backgrounding preserves the local match state and resumes at the same turn.
- Quitting an unfinished match discards it after an explicit confirmation; it does not count as a loss or affect a streak.
- A completed match can be replayed. Starting again never changes the prior result or the random distribution.
- A local result is not an online-verified result and cannot later be uploaded as one.

## Fairness and implementation contract for a later prototype

- Generate each die independently and uniformly from 1–6 using the platform's approved random source. Do not seed production outcomes from time, user identity, or a predictable counter. Do not change odds based on score, purchases, identity, engagement, or guide difficulty.
- Keep the rules engine pure and versioned. A complete turn input plus random outcomes must produce one deterministic state transition. Replaying the recorded event sequence must reproduce the match exactly.
- Enforce invariants: exactly five rounds; exactly two dice per throw; values always 1–6; at most one reroll of exactly one die per turn; score is the final dice sum; no negative score; exactly one terminal state after round five; a tie is a draw.
- In local-only MVP, match state remains on device. No backend or account is needed to play. If online play is added later, the server—not the client—must validate every turn and generate authoritative outcomes; online design requires its own reviewed fairness, privacy, abuse, and outage plan.
- Do not collect story reflections or sensitive religious/profile data for game telemetry. Proposed aggregate events are limited to tutorial completion, match start/completion/quit, chosen mode, duration, and ruleset version, subject to the app's analytics consent and retention rules.

## UX and content boundaries

- Keep the story and challenge as separate routes with separate progress. A player can read the sourced episode without playing, or play without repeatedly encountering the sensitive narrative.
- The current tutorial teaches only the interpreted five-round mechanics. Unlockable notes paraphrase source-audited claims and display the Critical Edition locator; obtain cultural/language review before public launch.
- Before the story reaches the humiliation and assembly debate, provide a concise content note, a skip-to-context action, and a way to exit. The playable challenge does not reproduce those scenes.
- Use plain names for actions: **Roll**, **Keep**, **Reroll one**, **Pause**, **How to play**. Avoid terms such as “wager,” “stake,” “fortune,” “wealth,” “virtue points,” or “karma score” in the game UI.
- Make the source/adaptation distinction visible in the entry card, rules sheet, and any share card—not only in a distant legal note.
- Share is off by default and user initiated. The initial release should share no personal data; if a result card is later added, offer anonymous sharing and preview the exact content before the OS share sheet opens.
- Reuse the app's established language selection only where reviewed translations of the rules and story exist. Clearly identify untranslated source text and use the existing explicit language fallback; do not silently substitute Hindi for Punjabi.
- Meet reduced-motion, screen-reader, text-scaling, contrast, and touch-target requirements. Dice values must be conveyed as text/accessibility labels, not color or animation alone.

## Prototype acceptance and test plan

Before visual polish or online work, test a low-fidelity playable prototype with a small mix of new and returning app users. Treat the results as formative usability evidence, not statistical proof of market fit.

### Must-pass functional checks

- A first-time player can explain that the presentation is traditional while the playable rules are Shoonaya interpretation, not the epic's documented rules.
- The player can complete or exit a match without a network connection or account.
- Keep and reroll choices work at every round boundary; repeated taps cannot consume multiple rerolls or score twice.
- Pausing, backgrounding, and reopening resumes the same state and event history.
- Replaying an event log reproduces totals and the outcome exactly.
- Guide and player follow the displayed rules; no mode alters the dice odds.
- Round five resolves to exactly one of win, loss, or draw; equal totals produce draw without extra turns.
- Screen-reader users can identify round, dice, available actions, subtotal, and outcome; reduced motion does not hide state changes.
- Reviewers do not interpret the challenge as Shakuni's historical procedure, a reenactment of Draupadi's humiliation, or a measure of spiritual merit.

### Signals to observe

- Rules comprehension before play and after the first turn.
- Tutorial completion, first-match completion, abandonment step, and replay intent.
- Perceived fairness and whether the guide's policy is understood.
- Whether five rounds feel appropriately brief and the reroll decision feels meaningful.
- Cultural comfort with the title, imagery, guide framing, and separation from the story.
- Accessibility issues on both iOS and Android, including text scaling and reduced motion.

Record the device/build, ruleset version, test task, observations, failures, and participant count. Do not announce numerical success claims from a tiny formative sample; use it to decide whether to simplify, revise, or proceed to a larger test.
