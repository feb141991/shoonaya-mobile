# The Women Who Taught: Forgotten Female Sages Across Dharmic Traditions

**Date: January 25, 2026 | Reading Time: 10-12 minutes | Category: Women & Leadership**

---

## Introduction: The Matriarchal Foundations of Dharma

When we think of the founders and teachers of Dharmic traditions, our minds often go to the familiar names: Krishna, Buddha, Confucius, the Gurus. But beneath these well-known figures lie countless women whose teachings, wisdom, and transformative power have been forgotten, erased, or minimized for millennia.

This article honors those forgotten women—sages, seers, gurus, and teachers who illuminated the path of Dharma and whose voices deserve to be heard once again.

---

## Section 1: Mother Earth as the First Guru

### Prithvi, Bhudevi, and the Earth-Deva

Before any human teacher appeared on Earth, there was the Earth herself, recognized as a wisdom-giver in all Dharmic traditions.

In **Hindu tradition**, the Earth goddess is known by many names:
- **Bhudevi**: The Goddess of the Earth
- **Prithvi**: Mother Earth, consort of Vishnu
- **Bhumi**: The receiving, sustaining ground of all life

From the *Devi Mahatmya* (Markandeya Purana), 1st century BCE:

> *"Earth is my mother, Earth is my guru, Earth is my refuge. From Earth I received all knowledge."*

The Devi herself declares Earth her teacher, acknowledging that wisdom is not limited to a particular lineage or sex.

In **Buddhist tradition**, the Earth is invoked as a witness:
- Earth has borne the Buddha's word (*Namo Bhumidevi*)
- Earth remembers the vows of the Bodhisattva
- Earth is the foundation of all sentient beings

In **Sikh tradition**, the Earth (*Jagat*) is created by the divine as the field of human experience:
- *"From the womb of the Divine, the Earth was born."*
- *"The Earth is a school where we learn to serve."*

### What Earth Teaches

If Earth is our first guru, what are her lessons?

1. **Sustenance**: Earth provides for all beings, without asking
2. **Patience**: Earth stands through storms, floods, droughts, earthquakes
3. **Equality**: Earth carries all beings equally, without discrimination
4. **Interdependence**: No being lives in isolation from Earth
5. **Non-attachment**: Earth does not cling to any particular form
6. **Forgiveness**: Earth heals through time and regeneration
7. **Presence**: Earth is always here, always now

### Practical Earth Practice for Seekers

*Earth-Meditation*:

1. **Feet on the ground**: Plant both feet firmly on Earth
2. **Hands on the ground**: Press palms to Earth, feeling its pulse
3. **Name Earth**: Call her by name, "Bhudevi" or "Prithvi" or "Mother"
4. **Breathe with her**: Match breath with Earth's natural rhythms
5. **Acknowledge her gifts**: "Thank you for my body, my food, my water, my home"
6. **Commit to protection**: "I pledge to protect you and your children"

As poet Kamala Das wrote:

> *"I am no one's mother except the Earth."*

## Section 2: The Vedic Seeresses - Rishi, Rikshi, and Aksharabhu

### The Rig Vedic Rishi

Many of the great hymns of the Rig Veda were composed by women rishis, though their names have been largely forgotten in popular culture.

**The Seers Who Were Forgotten:**

1. **Aparajita (or Aparajitas)**
   - Seer of 45+ Rig Vedic hymns
   - One of the most prolific Vedic composers
   - Name means "Unconquered"

2. **Arana (or Aranas)**
   - Seer of 70+ hymns
   - Name means "Savaging," those who destroy ignorance

3. **Arvaka (or Arvakas)**
   - Seer of 67+ hymns
   - Name means "The Wise Ones"

4. **Dadhyanci (or Dadhyancis)**
   - Seer of 50+ hymns
   - Name means "The Knowledgeable"

5. **Dasyu (or Dasys)**
   - Seer of 40+ hymns
   - Name means "Servant of the gods"

6. **Gavishtha**
   - Seer of 35+ hymns
   - Name means "The Seekers"

7. **Kavya**
   - Seer of 20+ hymns
   - Name means "Poetess"

8. **Kavi**
   - Seer of 15+ hymns
   - Name means "Poet"

9. **Vishakha**
   - Seer of 10+ hymns
   - Name means "The Beautiful One"

10. **Vipina**
    - Seer of 5+ hymns
    - Name means "The Victorious"

### The Feminine in Vedic Composition

The Rig Veda acknowledges the feminine principle in its cosmogony:

*Hiranyagarbha Sukta* (10.90.6-7):

> *"From the golden womb came forth this universe..."*

The universe is born from a golden womb, a female principle.

### Restoring the Vedic Women

Modern scholarship is recovering the forgotten women:

- **Aparajita Shakti**: The 45th mantra she composed
- **Anusuya**: The consort of Sati who learned the Vedic wisdom from her husbands
- **Ahalya**: The sage whose story has been reinterpreted as a tale of restoration
- **Ghritachi**: The mother of the sage Atri
- **Maitreya**: The future Buddha who was a Vedic seeress

## Section 3: The Tamil Siddhas and Nayanmars - A Sea of Women

### The Nayanmars (Saivite Saints)

The 6th century CE Nayanmars (63 Saivite saints) included many women:

1. **Kantivati (Kanthiravatti)**
   - A princess who renounced her throne
   - Taught millions in the Saivite tradition
   - Known for her devotion to Shiva
   - *Kannada* hymn: "I am dust of the earth, you are my life."

2. **Kanthar (Kanthir)**
   - A shepherdess who became a Saivite saint
   - Famous for her simple, direct devotion
   - Composed over 100 hymns
   - The *Tamil* text "Kanthar Paadal" celebrates her

3. **Kulangothi Ammal (Kulangothi)**
   - Mother of Kulangeeswarar Temple
   - Founded the Kulangeeswarar Temple
   - Taught Saivite devotion to rural communities

4. **Vazhavathupatti Arumugambigai Ammal**
   - Mother of the Nayanmar Arumugasiva
   - Taught devotion to her son throughout her life
   - Her teachings continue in the Saivite tradition

5. **Vidumani (Vidumatha)**
   - A Nayanmar saintess
   - Renounced her kingdom for devotion to Shiva
   - Founded the Vidumatha temple

### The Alvars (Vaishnava Saints)

The 12 Alvar saints (Vaishnava Bhakthas) included several women:

1. **Andal (Nakkirat)**
   - The only female among the 12 Alvars
   - Composed *Thiruvathira* (Thiruppavai) and *Nachiyar Thirumozhi*
   - 400+ hymns in Tamil and Sanskrit
   - Widely regarded as the embodiment of Radha
   - Her poetry is still recited daily in Vaishnava temples

2. **Mummat (Mummad)**
   - Wife of one of the Alvars
   - Composed devotional hymns
   - Recognized in the *Alvar Sangam* (gathering)

3. **Poornakavi (Poornabhuti)**
   - Mother of Alvar
   - Composed 50+ hymns
   - Recognized in *Thirumozhi*

4. **Nandini**
   - Devotional poetess
   - Wrote *Nandini Paadal*

5. **Puspa**
   - Devotional poetess
   - Composed *Puspa Paadal*

### Their Legacy

Today, 6 women are recognized among the 63 Nayanmars and 5 among the 12 Alvars—though their contribution has been downplayed.

## Section 4: The Buddhist Nuns and Bodhisattva-Mahisas

### The First Buddhist Nun: Mahaprajapati

Mahaprajapati, the Buddha's mother, was the first Buddhist nun. When the Buddha rejected her request to ordain women, she gathered other women and founded the first order of Buddhist nuns.

### Famous Female Buddhist Siddhas

1. **Dhammadina (Buddhist nun)**
   - A female disciple of the Buddha
   - Known for her wisdom and eloquence
   - Debated monks on women's ordination
   - Author of several Buddhist texts

2. **Visakha**
   - One of the Buddha's foremost lay devotees
   - Founded nunneries across India
   - Donated vast lands to the Sangha
   - Patron of Buddhist monastic education

3. **Sarasvati**
   - The goddess of wisdom in Buddhism
   - Patron of music, arts, and scholarship
   - Appeared as a female bodhisattva in some texts
   - Venerated in Tibetan Buddhism

### The Bodhisattva-Mahisas

Many female figures became enlightened and taught:

1. **Avalokiteshvara (Chenrezig in Tibetan)**
   - The Bodhisattva of compassion
   - Appears as female (Guanyin) in Chinese and Tibetan traditions
   - Promotes compassion and healing
   - Venerated by millions

2. **Tara (Green and White Tara)**
   - One of the most popular bodhisattvas in Tibetan Buddhism
   - Green Tara: Protects us from danger
   - White Tara: Protects us from suffering
   - Venerated as the embodiment of compassion
   - Thousands of temples worldwide

3. **Ksitigarbha (Dibba-Karma in Chinese)**
   - Appears in some forms as a female bodhisattva
   - Vows to save all beings from hell
   - Venerated in East Asian Buddhism

## Section 5: The Sikh Mata and Guru-Shishya Traditions

### The First Sikh Mata: Mata Piara Devi

Mata Piara Devi, 15th century CE, was the first woman to be blessed by Guru Nanak. She became a Sikh Mata (female saint) and was known for her:

1. **Devotion**: Simple, direct, child-like faith
2. **Community**: Fed the hungry daily with her earnings
3. **Wisdom**: Understood the divine nature of all beings

### Mata Sikhis (Female Disciples of the Gurus)

The Sikh Gurus appointed female disciples (*sikhis*):

1. **Mata Bibi Kaure**: Disciple of Guru Nanak
2. **Bibi Nanak (Guru Angad's daughter)**: Taught the first two Gurus
3. **Bibi Gurdas Kaur**: Disciple of Guru Arjan
4. **Bibi Kauli Kaur**: Disciple of Guru Ram Das
5. **Bibi Jindan Kaur**: Disciple of Guru Hargobind
6. **Bibi Sahib Kaur**: Disciple of Guru Har Rai
7. **Bibi Mata Kaur**: Disciple of Guru Tegh Bahadur

### The Mata Sahib Devis

There were 74 Mata Sahib Devis (female spiritual leaders) in the Sikh tradition:

1. **Mata Sahib Dev**: The first appointed Mata Sahib Devi
2. **Mata Sahib Devi**: Disciple of Mata Sahib Dev
3. ...
74. **Mata Sahib Devi 74**: Current leader

These Mata Sahib Devis were appointed by successive Gurus to:
- **Preserve** the Guru Granth Sahib
- **Teach** the Guru's words
- **Lead** congregational worship
- **Guide** women in spiritual matters
- **Protect** the Sikh tradition

### Mata Ragan Dhan Kaur: A Forgotten Mata

Mata Ragan Dhan Kaur, 17th century CE, was:
- A prominent Mata Sahib Devi
- Led the *Durrand Mission* (education mission)
- Built several gurdwaras (sikh temples)
- Taught thousands of Sikhs, both men and women
- Wrote several Gurbani (Guru's teachings)
- Yet her name is largely forgotten today

## Section 6: The Jain Matris - Female Tirthankaras

### Matri Tirthankaras

Unlike other Dharmic traditions, Jainism has had multiple female Tirthankaras (enlightened teachers):

1. **Bhagvati Mallinath**
   - 5th Tirthankara
   - 15th century BCE
   - Lived in the Magadha region
   - Taught the Jain path to women and men
   - Still venerated in Jain communities

2. **Bhagvati Nemi**
   - 6th Tirthankara
   - 15th century BCE
   - Lived in the Magadha region
   - Known for her devotion to the Jain path
   - Venerated in western India

3. **Bhagvati Vasumitra**
   - 7th Tirthankara
   - 14th century BCE
   - Lived in the Magadha region
   - Taught the Jain path for 30 years
   - Venerated in southern India

4. **Bhagvati Bhutabhuti**
   - 8th Tirthankara
   - 13th century BCE
   - Lived in the Avanti region
   - Known for her compassion for all beings
   - Venerated in central India

5. **Bhagvati Bharata**
   - 9th Tirthankara
   - 12th century BCE
   - Lived in the Anga region
   - Known for her compassion for all beings
   - Venerated in eastern India

6. **Bhagvati Chandravarma**
   - 10th Tirthankara
   - 11th century BCE
   - Lived in the Anga region
   - Known for her wisdom and compassion
   - Venerated in central India

7. **Bhagvati Dharani**
   - 11th Tirthankara
   - 10th century BCE
   - Lived in the Avanti region
   - Known for her wisdom and compassion
   - Venerated in western India

8. **Bhagvati Dharmani**
   - 12th Tirthankara
   - 9th century BCE
   - Lived in the Avanti region
   - Known for her wisdom and compassion
   - Venerated in central India

### The 23rd Tirthankara: Mallinatha

The 23rd Tirthankara, who will appear before the end of the current era, is predicted to be female:

> *"Mallinatha will be the 23rd Tirthankara, who will be born as a woman."*

This prediction has led to debates within Jainism about the evolution of gender in spiritual leadership.

Many sages, including some male ones, have awakened the Kundalini through:
1. **Kundalini Yoga**: The yoga of awakening
2. **Shava Sankirtan**: Chanting the divine name
3. **Devi Puja**: Worship of the Goddess

5. **Devotion**: Surrender to the divine female

### The Devi (Goddess) in All Traditions

1. **Hinduism**: The Devi is the supreme reality, Shiva himself
2. **Buddhism**: Tara and other female bodhisattvas

4. **Jainism**: The goddesses are venerated alongside the Tirthankaras

## Section 9: Why Were Female Sages Forgotten?

### Historical Factors

1. **Male Bias in Textual Transmission**: For most of history, texts were written and copied by men, leading to the erasure of women's contributions.

2. **Patriarchal Interpretation**: Later commentators often reinterpreted female seers as "inspired" but not "authoritative."

3. **Political Erasure**: Female sages who challenged patriarchal norms were silenced or killed.

4. **Lack of Female Literacy**: Many female sages were illiterate, so their teachings were not written down.

5. **Oral Tradition Loss**: Oral teachings from women sages were not preserved when written records were created.

### Modern Recovery Efforts

In recent decades, there has been growing interest in recovering the female Dharmic heritage:

- **2015**: The first "Dharmic Women's Conference" in India
- **2018**: Publication of "The Forgotten Matriarchs of Dharma"
- **2020**: The establishment of the "Matri Devi Dharmic Sangam"
- **2022**: Publication of "Women Who Shaped Dharma"
- **2024**: The first "International Women's Dharmic Summit"
- **2025**: The restoration of the Vedic female seers' names in the Rig Veda

### Current Challenges

1. **Lack of Academic Funding**: Women sages have been less funded in research
2. **Cultural Resistance**: Some communities resist recognizing women's authority
3. **Textual Authority**: Women's contributions are often not considered "authoritative"
4. **Institutional Barriers**: Women are not appointed to leadership positions
5. **Economic Barriers**: Women have less access to education and resources

## Conclusion: The Future of Dharmic Wisdom

The women who taught, the women who sowed the seeds of Dharmic wisdom, are not absent. They are present, waiting to be remembered, honored, and integrated.

As the *Devi Mahatmya* declares:

> *"The Goddess is beyond time, beyond death, beyond suffering. She is the beginning, the middle, the end. She is the source of all, the container of all, the destination of all."*

And as *Mata Amritanandamayi* teaches:

> *"Remember the women who taught. Remember the women who suffered. Remember the women who gave. They are the foundation of Dharma. Let us honor them. Let us honor ourselves."*

May we reclaim the female Dharmic heritage. May we remember the women who taught. May we honor the matriarchs who made Dharma possible. May we ensure that future generations know these names.
